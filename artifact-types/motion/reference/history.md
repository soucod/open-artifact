# History and real people

Read this with explainer.md when the film tells something that happened: a person's life or one day of it, a speech, a
discovery, how a place or an organisation came to be, an anniversary. The viewer most likely knows neither the person
nor the time, so the film first lets them see the situation, in seconds, and only then says why it matters. It is no
lesson and no row of slides.

Nothing here is a template. One film is a speech of the 1920s with two photographs to its name, the next a founder's
first workshop, the next a writer's letter: the telling, the backbone of the picture, the palette and the layout
should differ each time. explainer.md's rules on facts, on one thing at a time and on reading it back all hold.

## 1. Four things the viewer must have

Who this is. What the situation was. Why this moment: why it was said or done, and to whom. What came of it. Every one
is in the film, in any order: it can open on the moment itself, and "what came of it" can be the last line. In 20 to
30 seconds each gets one picture; more seconds add evidence, not more topics.

## 2. The backbone comes from what there is to show

Look at what carries most among the things they gave you, build the film on that, and weave the rest in:

| What carries most | The backbone | Woven in |
|---|---|---|
| Their photographs of the person, the place, the event | The photographs: a slow push, one detail enlarged, several set down on a table one by one | A map for the place, a line for the date |
| Few pictures or none (an early period) | Drawn: a map and a route, a timeline, a headline that sets line by line, a document, a figure that grows | Their one or two pictures, kept for frame 0 and for the beat that matters most |
| A writer's or a scholar's words | Type: the quotation arriving phrase by phrase on a quiet page | The place, the book, the desk |
| A meeting, a negotiation, a campaign | A map and who stood where: positions, routes, dates | The headlines of those days |
| A clip of theirs | The clip, as muted picture (explainer.md, "Their real things as evidence") | A map, a name and a date beneath |

- **Every beat moves.** A photograph pushes in, a line travels the map, a headline sets, a figure grows, the words of
  a quotation light one by one. A card lies over the picture; it is never the ground.
- **Frame 0 is a finished cover:** the person or the place, the name, the occasion, the year. With the sound off it
  still says who and when.
- **Their words early.** One sentence the person really said, within the first seconds, is the strongest way in: the
  words arrive one by one, with who said them and when.
- **The last beat hands on:** to what came of it, or to the thing itself where the person will play it elsewhere. A
  film here cannot hold their recording (voice.md says what to tell them): it gives the setting, and the words on
  screen.

## 3. The look comes from the period

Its paper, its type, its print: newsprint and metal type, a telegram form, a typewritten sheet, a ledger, a survey
map. The feel of a period comes from the tone of the paper, the letterforms and the texture, never from type made
small or faint: sizes and contrast stay as motion.md says, and what must be read is large. No style of a regime's, an
army's or a movement's posters beyond what the subject needs, and motion.md's never-draw list holds, with the one
allowance it makes for an exhibit in a real history film. The look is not carried over from the last history film.

## 4. Real and drawn stay apart

- **A real person is seen only in a real picture that the person you work with gave you.** Never a drawn likeness
  passed off as them, and never a photograph "restored", colorised or changed. With no picture, the person is a name,
  a date and their own words, or a small figure in the film's own hand, as motion.md draws people; a portrait they ask
  for is plainly a drawing.
- **A reconstruction is labelled as one:** a drawn room, street or object that stands for the real one carries a
  small "illustration" beside it.
- **A map shows coastlines and rivers.** Fronts, routes and areas held are marked as approximate, and no disputed
  border is drawn.
- **Each picture's source stands in a small credit,** clear of the words. Nothing is fetched from elsewhere into the
  film (SKILL.md).

## 5. The words

- **A quotation is exact,** with who said it and when, and comes from what they gave you or a source you read.
  Nothing is put into a real person's mouth, and nobody is shown endorsing what they did not (questions.md).
- **Every date, figure, title and name is checked.** Where you can look things up, hold each fact that matters
  against two sources, and make sure it is the right occasion: the same person often spoke more than once, and titles
  and dates get crossed. What you can't settle is left out, or said to be uncertain.
- **No verdict and no stirring words.** Say what happened and let the viewer judge; add no motive or feeling that the
  source doesn't give.
- **Names as they gave them.** Honorifics, calendars, and the names of places and bodies that are in dispute follow
  the person's own usage. Without it, take the plain form that can be checked (the person's name, the common-era
  date, the body's usual short name), and don't choose a side for them.
- A name is written in full the first time and may be short after that.

## 6. Read it back

- Could someone who has never heard of this person say, after frame 0 and the first scene, who it is and when?
- Are all four things there?
- Does every beat move, with no card as the ground?
- Is every real face a real picture, every reconstruction labelled, every quotation exact and attributed?
- Is the type as large and as clear as in any other film, with the period in the paper and the letters?
- Does it look like this period and this person, not like the last history film?
